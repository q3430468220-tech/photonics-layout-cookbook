A rib waveguide keeps a thin slab of silicon on both sides of its core. The etch stops 90 nm above the buried oxide instead of going through. The slab is what an active device needs: it carries the implants and the contacts of a modulator to the core. This chapter draws a rib waveguide with the PDK and finds what the slab costs: a weaker hold on the light, and so a larger bend.

The ring modulator of chapter 4 is built on this waveguide. Its step 2 computes the same cross-section, and the numbers here agree with it.

The device #

devices/rib_waveguide_builder.py draws a straight, an S-bend and a straight in a row, and has a cell for each part: straight and bend. From the repository root:

shell
uv run python devices/rib_waveguide_builder.py

rib 0.5 um wide on a 6 um slab, S-bend of R = 10 um, route 45.708 um
GDS -> devices/gds/rib_waveguide.gds   ports: ['o1', 'o2']
DRC passed: 0 errors, 0 warnings
parameter default where it comes from
width 0.5 µm core width, the same as the strip
slab_width 6 µm wide enough for a contact on each side of the core
radius 10 µm the rib bend radius of the design rules; step 1 shows why it is twice that of the strip

The core is drawn on the layer WG and the slab on SLAB90. In the process file the two are grown from the same level, 220 nm and 90 nm high, so the core stands 130 nm above the slab.

Step 1. The modes, by MODE #

The question What does the rib guide at 1.55 µm, and how does it compare with the strip of the same width?

The model. A straight 6 µm piece of the waveguide, written to GDS and loaded into a Layer Builder with the process file. The mode solver uses a window of 8 µm by 2.4 µm, so that the whole 6 µm slab lies inside it, with perfectly matched layers on every side. The mesh is 20 nm, and 10 nm over the rib, with a mesh line on every silicon surface: the top of the core, the top of the slab, the bottom, and the two sidewalls.

What "guided" means in a rib. In a strip, a mode is guided when its index is above that of the oxide. In a rib there is silicon beside the core, and light can also leave sideways through the slab. The slab alone guides a mode of its own, with an index of 2.1035 for a 90 nm slab between oxide. A mode of the rib stays at the core only while its index is above that. The script computes the slab index from the three-layer slab equation, with the indices it reads from the structure, and uses it as the threshold.

Reading the figure.

  • a, the mode. The light sits over the core and spreads into the slab on both sides. The index is higher than in the strip, 2.568 against 2.444, because there is more silicon under the mode.
  • b, the bend. The mode moves outwards, away from the centre of the bend, by 26 nm at 10 µm. The grey line marks the middle of the core.
  • c, dispersion. The group index is 3.94, lower than the 4.24 of the strip: a weaker confinement gives a weaker dispersion.
  • d, the bend. As in the strip, the loss of a bend is at its two ends, where the shifted mode meets the mode of the straight. The shift is larger here, so the loss is larger: 0.046 dB at 10 µm, and 0.28 dB at 5 µm, where radiation also begins.
  • e, the width. The line is the slab mode. TE1 crosses it between 0.45 and 0.50 µm.

What it tells you. The slab loosens the hold of the waveguide on the light sideways. The same 0.5 µm core that bends at 5 µm as a strip needs 10 µm as a rib for a comparable loss, and that is where the design rule puts it. This is the trade of an active process: use ribs where the device needs the slab, and strips for the routing between devices.

Checks.

  • Against the ring modulator. Step 2 of chapter 4 simulated this cross-section as part of the ring and found neff = 2.56770 and ng = 3.9370 for the straight waveguide, and 2.57016 at R = 10 µm. This chapter, with its own layout and its own script, finds the same numbers.
  • The group index. From the slope of neff(λ) it is 3.916; the solver gives 3.937, 0.5 % apart.

Pitfalls.

  • The threshold must be the slab mode. With the oxide index as the threshold, the solver's list contains modes of the slab that happen to have some power near the core. An early run of this script reported a "TE1" at 0.30 µm with an index of 1.74, far below the slab mode. It was a slab mode.
  • The mesh. Without a mesh line on the slab surface the index of this waveguide changes by 0.007 with the size of the window. Chapter 4 has the full story: the mesh, and why it decides the answer.

Limits #

  • Smooth, vertical sidewalls and a flat slab. Scattering loss is not modelled.
  • The slab is undoped here. In a modulator the implants add loss; chapter 4 computes it.
  • The bend is a uniform arc between two straights, at 1.55 µm.
  • The numbers are for a teaching process and have not been compared with a fabricated device.

Exercises #

  1. A thinner slab. A shallower rib etch leaves a thicker slab. In gsip_process_file_py/gsip_csv/process_layers.csv the slab is 90 nm; note what you would have to change in gsip_technology.py as well, and why the tests would catch a change in only one of them.
  2. The slab width. Run the builder with slab_width=3.0 and simulate it. Does neff change? At what slab width does the mode start to feel the edge of the slab?
  3. A wider core. Run waveguide_modes_fde.py --widths 0.6 0.7 0.8 --radii 10. How far above the slab mode is TE1 at 0.8 µm, and what does that mean for a bend?
