This chapter draws one device from first principles. The device is a microring modulator. A ring is a closed rib waveguide next to a straight bus. A PN junction in the ring changes the refractive index when a voltage is applied, and a heater above the ring shifts the resonance with heat. The layout comes from one Python file, devices/ring_modulator_builder.py, which uses the process defined in chapter 1, gsip_technology.py.

By the end you should be able to:

  • say why every polygon of the device is where it is;
  • change the radius, the gap or the angles, and keep the device clean in the design-rule check;
  • write a builder for your own device in the same style;
  • run a design-rule check (DRC) and read its output.

Two words first. A DRC is a program that measures a layout against a list of rules, such as the minimum width of a wire or the enclosure of a via. A GDS file is the standard binary file that holds mask layouts. Each polygon in it sits on a layer, identified by two numbers, layer/datatype.

Commands in this chapter run from the repository root, with the environment made by uv.

1. The device in one picture #

Read the device as a stack of jobs. Each part has one job, and each job uses one or two layers.

part layers job
ring WG, SLAB90, N, P, NP, PP, NPP, PPP Guides the light around a 10 µm circle. The arc of 240° carries the PN junction.
bus WG, SLAB90 The straight waveguide that feeds light past the ring. Ports o1 (in) and o2 (through).
coupler WG, SLAB90 (where the two slabs meet) The 0.37 µm gap where the ring and bus cores run close enough to exchange light.
contact vias VIAC Connect the implants of the junction to metal 1.
metal 1 disc and arc M1 The electrodes of the junction. The disc inside the ring is signal (S). The arc outside is ground (G).
heater HEATER A TiN film above the core, 270° of arc. Resistive, so current through it heats the core.
via 1 VIA1 Connects metal 1 and the heater to metal 2.
metal 2 M2 Routes the electrodes and the heater to the pads, 6 µm wide.
via 2 VIA2 Connects metal 2 to the pads.
pads M3, PADOPEN Five 80 µm pads for probing or bonding. G S G in the north, H H in the south.

The layer numbers are in the LayerMapGSiP class of gsip_technology.py, which chapter 1 explains. The layer names in this table are the names the code uses.

2. The design decisions #

A layout is a list of decisions. For each decision the table gives the value, the reason and the place where the decision is tested later in the course. Read the reason as the question a student would ask. The simulation column points to the chapter 4 step that checks the decision.

decision value reason tested later
ring radius 10 µm (circumference 62.83 µm) The rule rib_bend_radius in DESIGN_RULES is 10 µm. It is a teaching value, and the builder refuses a smaller ring. Tighter bends lose much more light. Step 2: bend loss is 15.2 dB/cm at 4 µm, 0.56 dB/cm at 5 µm, and below the solver floor from 6 µm up.
rib waveguide 0.5 µm core, 220 nm, on a 90 nm slab 6 µm wide A rib keeps a slab for the implants and the contacts. Step 2: at 0.5 µm the second TE mode (TE1) is guided, but barely: neff 2.115 against the slab mode 2.104.
PN junction 240° of the ring, centred on north, 41.89 µm (67 %) Most of the ring is modulated, the bottom stays free for the bus. Step 3: the junction is 5 nm from the drawn position.
coupler undoped no implant at the bottom of the ring The implants stop short of the bus side, so the coupler region has no doping. Steps 1 and 6: the coupling and the loss are computed for the layout as drawn.
n inside, p outside n (signal) inside the ring, p (ground) outside The signal disc is the inner electrode, the ground arc is the outer one. Step 3: n core at 4.977e17 against 5e17 in the process.
contact vias 0.5 µm squares, near edge 1.9 µm from the core centre line The via sits 0.65 µm inside the N++ edge and 0.6 µm from the slab edge, so both margins hold. Step 3: the doping seen by the solver matches the process within 0.5 %.
metal 1 disc and arc disc edge 1.6 µm inside the core centre line, arc from 1.6 µm to 3.6 µm outside The metal keeps 0.8 µm from the heater, and the contact vias lie inside the metal. Step 4: the electrodes cost 21 % of the heater's efficiency.
TiN heater arc 1.6 µm wide above the core, 270°, free filament 44.72 µm, 280 Ω The free filament is 71 % of the ring: the 270° arc less the two landings. The landings are where via 1 connects it. Step 4: 2.35 K/mW in the heated section. Step 5: 123.6 pm/mW for the ring.
metal 1 to heater distance 0.8 µm in the layout, 0.6 µm rule The two films lie in one plane. They must not touch or come close. DRC: 0 errors (section 7).
metal 2 routing 6 µm wide wires, S crosses the ring The signal must reach the pad in the north, across the heater and metal 1. Layer order: metal 2 is above both.
GSG pads 80 µm, 100 µm pitch, 95 µm from the centre Three pads in a row give a ground-signal-ground arrangement for the junction. Not simulated: the circuit model has no RF model of pads.
two heater pads H1, H2 south, 80 µm The heater needs two contacts, one at each landing. Step 4: 10 mW needs 1.67 V and 6.0 mA in the free filament.

The question "why is the heater not on metal 1?" comes up first. The answer is in the process. The heater is a thin film (TiN, 0.15 µm thick, from z = 1.10 µm to 1.25 µm). Metal 1 is an aluminium layer of 0.7 µm, from z = 1.10 µm to 1.80 µm. Both start on the same oxide surface. A heater is a resistor: current must flow through its filament from one landing to the other. If metal 1 touched the heater, it would short the filament. The builder keeps 0.8 µm between the two, and the DRC requires 0.6 µm. This is why the metal 1 electrodes stop short of the heater, and why the heater is an arc of its own.

The other question is "why two descriptions of one process?" The layout needs geometry and rules. The simulator needs thicknesses, materials and doping. Chapter 3 writes the same process as tables for the simulator. Both use the same numbers. Chapter 1 explains where those numbers come from.

3. How the builder is organised #

devices/ring_modulator_builder.py has five cells and a set of helpers. Each cell is a function decorated with @gf.cell. A cell is built once and cached. The cells are:

cell what it draws ports
ring the closed ring, the slab of the ring and the six implants none
bus a straight rib waveguide, 120 µm long, centred on the origin o1, o2
pn_contacts the contact vias, metal 1 disc and arc, and via 1 on the disc and the arc S, G1, G2 (on metal 2)
heater the TiN arc and its two landings, with via 1 on each landing H1, H2 (on metal 2)
ring_modulator assembles the four cells above, places the bus, draws the metal 2 traces and the pads G1, S, G2, H1, H2 (on metal 3), o1, o2

The device cell is the one you call. The others are its parts. Here is how ring_modulator assembles them. These lines are quoted from the file:

python
c = gf.Component()
c << ring(radius=radius, width=width, pn_angle=pn_angle, p_inside=p_inside)
b = c << bus(length=bus_length, width=width)
b.dmovey(y_bus)
e = c << pn_contacts(radius=radius, pn_angle=pn_angle)
h = c << heater(radius=radius, heater_angle=heater_angle)

The bus is moved down with dmovey(y_bus). y_bus is the height of the bus centre line, -(radius + width + gap). The top edge of the bus core then sits gap below the outer edge of the ring core. The gap is the edge-to-edge distance.

The ring cell builds its geometry in one extrusion:

python
a0, a1, _, _ = _angles(pn_angle, 0)
passive = gf.get_cross_section("rib", width=width, width_slab=SLAB_WIDTH, radius=radius)
loop = gf.path.extrude(gf.Path(_arc_points(radius, 269.5, 630.5)), cross_section=passive)

_angles turns the angles around north into start and end angles. Read it once. The angle 90° is north, and each angle is measured counter-clockwise from the east. A pn_angle of 240° gives a0 = -30 and a1 = 210, an arc from south-east to south-west through north.

Helpers that you will reuse in your own builder:

  • _arc_points(radius, a0, a1) returns points on a circle, at 0.5° steps.
  • _sector(r0, r1, a0, a1) returns an annular sector, the shape of a heater arc.
  • _via_row(c, layer, radius, a0, a1, size, pitch) places squares along an arc (section 5).

Two more are in devices/wiring.py, which the builders of the library share:

  • box(centre, radial, tangential, angle) returns a rectangle that points along an angle.
  • wire(c, points, start_reach, end_reach=0.0, width=6.0) draws a metal 2 wire through a list of points (section 5).

4. The layers, in the order of the figure #

The figure is built in the order a process is built: silicon first, then contacts, then the metals. Read each panel with the question "what does this layer do?"

Panel a, the whole device. Five pads of metal 3 (light squares), the metal 2 traces that connect them, and the ring with its bus in the middle. The thin inner outline of each pad is the passivation opening (PADOPEN). The letters G, S and H name the ports. The in and through labels mark o1 and o2.

Panel b, silicon and implants. The grey slab is SLAB90, the 90 nm slab of the rib. The core (WG) is drawn as an outline on top of it. The six implants are opaque and nested. On the n side, going outwards from the core: N (light blue), then N+ (NP), then N++ (NPP, dark blue). The p side is mirrored: P (light red), then P+ (PP), then P++ (PPP, dark red). N covers the whole n side, N+ covers everything beyond 0.65 µm from the core centre, and N++ covers everything beyond 1.25 µm. The nesting follows the cross-section rib_pn in gsip_technology.py.

Panel c, contacts, metal 1 and heater. Two rows of black squares are the contact vias (VIAC): one inside the ring, one outside. Metal 1 (yellow) is the disc inside and the arc outside. The purple arc is the heater (HEATER). Its landings reach out at the ends. The heater and metal 1 start at the same height, z = 1.10 µm, but they are separate films, and the 0.8 µm distance keeps them apart.

Panel d, via 1 and metal 2. Dark squares are via 1 (VIA1). They land on metal 1 at the disc and the arc, and on the heater landings. Metal 2 (orange) carries the signal straight up over the ring, and the two ground wires sideways above the outer electrode, then up to their pads. The two heater wires leave the landings downwards, below this window. Panel a shows them crossing the bus to the heater pads.

Panel e, all layers. Every layer stacked, bottom to top, with opaque fills, so that a layer above hides the layers below it where they overlap. Metal 2 is drawn as an outline here so that the ring and the crossings stay visible. The outline of the signal wire crosses the heater, and the outlines of the heater wires cross the bus at the bottom.

Metal 2 lies from z = 2.30 µm to 3.00 µm, which is 2.1 µm above the top of the core. The signal wire crosses the ring at that height, and the heater wires cross the bus. The figure is a plan view, so it cannot show this. The absorption of the metal 2 wires, and of the heater and the metal 1 electrodes, is not modelled in any simulation of this course. The mode solver window ends 0.88 µm of oxide above the core, below these metals, and the optical results hold only for the structure as the simulations draw it.

Because the figure shows one device, not a process, the colours follow the PDK colours of the layer table. The table in panel a is the legend for all five panels.

5. Lessons the code carries #

The comments and tests in the code teach more than the parameters do. Four of them are worth reading closely.

Exact polygons where the slabs meet, not a boolean clean-up. The ring slab is an annulus, and the bus slab is a band. Where the outer circle of the ring crosses the upper edge of the bus slab, the two outlines separate at a shallow angle. They leave a thin wedge of missing slab. The wedge is narrower than the minimum space of 0.3 µm, so the DRC would flag it. The function _slab_fillets fills each wedge with an explicit four-point polygon. The fillet overlaps both slabs, so that the merged outline has no seam.

python
for fillet in _slab_fillets(radius, y_bus):
    c.add_polygon(fillet, layer=t.SLAB90)
c.flatten()
slab = c.get_region(t.SLAB90, merge=True)

Why not let a boolean operation clean up the wedge? A union of two slabs gives an outline with many small vertices, and the result depends on the tolerance. Explicit fillets give the same outline each time, and a reader can check the numbers in the code.

An odd number of vias, so that one sits on the symmetry axis. The cross-section figures of chapter 4 cut the device at x = 0. A via must be on that line to show in every row. _via_row computes how many squares fit along the arc, then makes the count odd. An even count would leave the middle of the row empty:

python
count = max(int(length // pitch), 1)
count -= 1 - count % 2
start = (length - (count - 1) * pitch) / 2

The first line counts the squares that fit. The second line subtracts one when the count is even. The third line centres the row on the arc. The via centres are at 7.85 µm and 12.15 µm from the ring centre. Their near edges are 1.9 µm from the core centre line at 10 µm. The test test_a_contact_via_sits_on_the_symmetry_axis checks that both rows have a via at x = 0.

Constant-width Manhattan wires on metal 2. wire draws each segment as a rectangle of the same width, with square corners. A diagonal segment is refused with an error. A wire of one width along its whole length is easy to check, and its width matches the width of the port.

python
if abs(x1 - x0) > 1e-9 and abs(y1 - y0) > 1e-9:
    raise ValueError("a wire segment must be horizontal or vertical")

The two free ends of a wire need care. A wire that ran half its width past its last via 1 can come too close to a neighbour. In one tested variant with a 120° junction, such a wire came to 0.83 µm from its neighbour, below the 1.0 µm metal 2 space. So each free end stops where the enclosure rule needs it to stop, plus 0.1 µm. The function reach_beyond measures that distance from the corners of the via 1 squares, rotated squares included.

One more lesson in the ring itself. The ring is extruded as one closed path, from 269.5° to 630.5°. The path overlaps itself by 1°, and the result is merged. This removes the seam where a closed path starts and ends. The implant arcs are extruded separately and then clipped to the slab, because the end faces of two extrusions can differ by 1 nm.

6. Parameters and what the builder rejects #

ring_modulator takes these parameters. Each one has a default that matches the simulated device.

parameter default what it changes checked by
radius 10.0 µm ring radius, centre line at least the rib bend radius of 10 µm
width 0.5 µm core width of ring and bus none directly
gap 0.37 µm edge-to-edge gap between ring and bus at least the minimum space of 0.2 µm
pn_angle 240.0° the doped arc, centred on north heater clearance to the outer electrode
heater_angle 270.0° the heater arc, centred on north heater clearance to the electrode and to the bus
p_inside False False: n inside. True: p inside. none
bus_length 120.0 µm length of the bus, ports at its ends none
pad_size 80.0 µm pad edge length none
pad_pitch 100.0 µm spacing of pads within a row none
pad_distance 95.0 µm distance of the pad centres from the ring centre none

The builder refuses four kinds of input. Each one raises a ValueError, with a message that tells you what to change. The checks run in this order, and the first one that fails raises.

  • radius ... is below the rib bend radius. The radius must be at least 10 µm, the value of DESIGN_RULES["rib_bend_radius"]. For radius=8.0 the message is radius 8.0 um is below the rib bend radius, 10.0 um (gsip_technology.DESIGN_RULES['rib_bend_radius']).
  • heater_angle must exceed pn_angle by more. The outer electrode of metal 1 ends at pn_angle. The heater landing must stay clear of it by at least 0.8 µm (the rule of 0.6 µm plus 0.2 µm of margin). For pn_angle=240.0 and heater_angle=240.0 the message is heater_angle must exceed pn_angle by more: -1.20 um between the outer electrode and a heater landing, need 0.80 um. The message gives the clearance it found.
  • gap ... is below the minimum space of the waveguide layer. The gap must be at least 0.2 µm. For gap=0.15 the message is gap 0.15 um is below the minimum space of the waveguide layer.
  • the clearance between the heater and the bus core is .... The heater is drawn as a polygon, with its two landings, and the lowest point of that polygon must lie at least 0.5 µm above the top edge of the bus core. The message gives the measured clearance, the rule, the lowest point of the heater and the top of the bus core. For pn_angle=280.0 and heater_angle=320.0 it reads: the clearance between the heater and the bus core is -1.44 um, the rule needs 0.50 um: the lowest point of the heater (heater_angle 320.0, radius 10.0) is at y = -12.06 um, and the top of the bus core, set by the radius and the gap 0.37 um, is at y = -10.62 um.

At the defaults the two clearance checks leave these margins. The metal 1 arc and the nearest heater landing are 1.84 µm apart, against 0.8 µm required. The lowest point of the heater is at y = −9.62 µm, and the top of the bus core at y = −10.62 µm, so the heater clears the bus by 1.00 µm, against 0.50 µm required. With the pn angle fixed at 240° and a gap of 0.37 µm, the heater angle can grow until the bus check fails, at about 277.6°. It can shrink until the electrode check fails, at about 259.8°. Between these two limits the device builds. At a gap of 0.2 µm the bus clearance is 0.83 µm, so that gap also builds with the default heater.

The builder does not check the metal 2 wires against each other or against the ring. Only the DRC does that, on the finished layout.

7. The design-rule check #

A DRC does not know what a device is for. It knows rules. gsip_drc.py has a handful of them, the ones a device builder trips over first. A foundry's rule deck has hundreds of rules. This one is small on purpose, so that "the layout passes DRC" means something in the exercises.

The check covers four families of rules:

  1. Width and space of each mask layer, from MIN_WIDTH_SPACE. For example, the core must be at least 0.2 µm wide and at least 0.2 µm from another core. The heater film must be 0.8 µm wide.
  2. Enclosures. A contact via must lie inside N++ or P++ with 0.2 µm of margin, and inside metal 1 with 0.1 µm. Via 1 must lie inside metal 1 or the heater with 0.2 µm, and inside metal 2 with 0.2 µm. Via 2 and the pad opening have their own margins.
  3. Rules between layers. Metal 1 and the heater must not touch and must keep 0.6 µm. N++ and P++ must not overlap, and N and P must not overlap.
  4. Warnings, which do not fail the check. An implant drawn where there is no silicon, and an N++ or P++ implant closer than 0.4 µm to a waveguide core (optical loss).

The rules are read from DESIGN_RULES and MIN_WIDTH_SPACE in gsip_technology.py. The DRC does not hold a copy of the numbers, so a change of a rule in one place changes the check. The check holds two small tolerances of its own, which the code explains: a 5 nm tolerance on the silicon outlines, and a 1 nm shrink on the N and P overlap, so that the junction itself does not count as an overlap.

Run it on a component. This is the usual way. The check reads the component directly:

python
from devices import ring_modulator_builder as rm
from gsip_drc import run_drc

device = rm.ring_modulator()
result = run_drc(device)
print(result.summary())

The default device prints DRC passed: 0 errors, 0 warnings. Pass rdb_path= to save the markers, as the builder does with build/drc/ring_modulator.lyrdb.

Run it on a GDS file. This is the way to check any layout, including one from another tool:

shell
uv run python gsip_drc.py devices/gds/ring_modulator.gds

The check writes ring_modulator_drc.lyrdb next to the file. The exit code is 0 when the layout passes.

What the output means. The summary has one line for each rule that fired, with the number of places where it fired. The count is of distinct places, not of edge pairs. A rule that fires at one place counts as 1, even when the check finds several edges there.

DRC FAILED: 7 errors, 1 warnings
  error       1  WG: narrower than 0.2 um
  error       1  WG: space smaller than 0.2 um
  error       1  contact via not enclosed by metal 1 with 0.1 um margin
  error       1  via 1 not enclosed by metal 1 or the heater with 0.2 um margin
  error       1  via 1 not enclosed by metal 2 with 0.2 um margin
  error       1  metal 1 closer than 0.6 um to the heater film
  error       1  N++ overlaps P++
  warning     1  implant drawn where there is no silicon

This is the actual output of the self-test. The self-test is the second way to run the check.

The self-test with planted violations. gsip_drc.py --demo builds a small layout with one violation per rule family, and checks that the DRC finds them. Each line in the output above corresponds to a planted shape:

  • a core 0.15 µm wide (narrower than 0.2 µm);
  • two cores 0.1 µm apart (space smaller than 0.2 µm);
  • a contact via whose metal 1 reaches only 0.05 µm beyond it on one side;
  • a via 1 that sticks out of the heater, with no metal 2 above it and no metal 1 under it;
  • a metal 1 shape 0.2 µm from the heater;
  • an N++ implant that overlaps a P++ implant;
  • an N implant with no silicon under it (the warning).

The self-test passes when at least seven error rules fire and the warning fires too.

Reading the marker file in KLayout. The DRC writes a KLayout report database (.lyrdb). Open the layout in KLayout and load the report in KLayout's marker browser. Each marker sits on the place where the rule failed. Each rule is a category, named error: <rule> or warning: <rule>. Use the markers when a count tells you that a rule failed but not where.

A lone via row is a good first case to check. The snippet below draws nine contact vias on an arc, with no implant and no metal around them, and runs the check:

python
import gdsfactory as gf
import gsip_technology as t
from devices import ring_modulator_builder as rm
from gsip_drc import run_drc

@gf.cell
def contact_arc(radius: float = 10.0, a0: float = 60.0, a1: float = 120.0) -> gf.Component:
    c = gf.Component()
    c.info["vias"] = rm._via_row(c, t.VIAC, radius, a0, a1, rm.VIA_SIZE, rm.VIA_PITCH)
    return c

print(run_drc(contact_arc()).summary())

It prints DRC FAILED: 18 errors, 0 warnings, with one line for each of the two contact rules: nine places for contact via not enclosed by N++ / P++ with 0.2 um margin and nine for contact via not enclosed by metal 1 with 0.1 um margin. Each of the nine vias fails both rules. This is the point of the check: a via is not wrong by itself, but a via without the implant and the metal around it is.

8. Writing the GDS for the simulator #

Chapter 4 simulates the device in Lumerical. Lumerical reads one cell of one flat GDS file. The function write_gds_for_lumerical in gsip_technology.py writes that file. It places the component in a new cell, flattens it, names the cell and writes the file without the metadata cell that gdsfactory adds. The builder calls it at its end, through write_device in devices/__init__.py. Every builder of the device library ends with that function: it writes the layout, runs the DRC and draws a preview image.

python
gds = t.write_gds_for_lumerical(component, GDS / f"{name}.gds", cellname=name)

Run the builder to make the file:

shell
uv run python devices/ring_modulator_builder.py

The command writes devices/gds/ring_modulator.gds, the DRC report build/drc/ring_modulator.lyrdb, and two preview images in build/previews/. It prints the lengths the simulations need, the file it wrote and the DRC summary:

ring 62.832 um, PN section 41.888 um, heater 44.724 um
GDS -> devices/gds/ring_modulator.gds   ports: ['G1', 'S', 'G2', 'H1', 'H2', 'o1', 'o2']
DRC passed: 0 errors, 0 warnings

The process file (chapter 3) and the GDS are loaded together into the Layer Builder. The GDS gives the shapes, the process file gives the materials and the thicknesses. Neither file has the other's numbers. The simulation scripts contain no thickness, no material and no doping.

The info dictionary carries the lengths the simulations need: length_ring, length_pn and length_heater. The heater length is the free filament. It is the arc less half a landing width (1.2 µm) at each end, 2.4 µm in all. The dictionary also holds radius, width and gap. The figure script reads the lengths from info, so they are computed once from the parameters.

9. The tests that guard the layout #

tests/test_layout.py has eleven tests, and tests/test_connectivity.py has four test cases (three functions, one of them run for two values of p_inside). They run without Lumerical. Run them from the root:

shell
uv run pytest tests/test_layout.py
uv run pytest tests/test_connectivity.py

Each test guards one claim about the device.

test what it guarantees
test_default_ring_modulator_is_drc_clean The default device has no errors and no warnings, and the DRC writes its report.
test_ports_and_info The seven ports exist, and the lengths in info match the geometry.
test_device_uses_the_whole_back_end Sixteen layers are used, so no layer of the back end is left out by accident.
test_gap_is_the_edge_to_edge_distance The gap is measured edge to edge, at 0.25 µm and 0.37 µm.
test_a_contact_via_sits_on_the_symmetry_axis Both via rows have a via at x = 0, where the figure cuts the device.
test_other_sizes_stay_drc_clean Radius 12, gap 0.25, angles 220 and 260, and p_inside=True all stay clean.
test_rejected_parameters The refusals of section 6 are raised, with their messages.
test_heater_clearance_is_measured_on_the_drawn_heater At a gap of 0.2 µm the device builds clean.
test_parameter_sweep_builds_clean_or_is_refused A grid of radius, gap, angles and p_inside: each variant builds DRC clean with intact nets, or is refused with a stated reason.
test_drc_finds_the_planted_violations The self-test finds the planted violations.
test_drc_reads_a_gds_file The DRC gives the same result on a GDS file as on the component.
test_pads_reach_the_junction_and_the_heater Traced through the vias, the signal pad reaches the inner implant and the ground pads reach the outer one, and the heater pads reach the heater film. Run for both polarities.
test_three_nets_and_no_short The two ground pads share one net, the two heater pads share one, and signal, ground and heater are three different nets.
test_every_via_lands_on_both_levels No via touches only one of the two levels it connects.

test_connectivity.py does not look at the shapes alone. It follows the conductors through the vias listed in LAYER_CONNECTIVITY in gsip_technology.py. A DRC cannot see a wrong connection, for example a signal pad wired to the ground implant. This test can.

When you change the builder, run the tests first. A failure in one test tells you which claim broke. The default device test is the main one: if it fails, a rule of the process is broken.

Check yourself #

  1. Why does the heater sit on its own arc instead of sharing the arc of metal 1? Give the reason from the process, not from the code.
  2. What happens to the layout if the bus is moved so that the gap is 0.15 µm? Which error message does the builder give, and where is the minimum set?
  3. Why does the builder place an odd number of contact vias on each row? Which test checks it?
  4. Where does a 0.6 µm rule between metal 1 and the heater come from, and which file holds it?
  5. A GDS file from another tool fails the DRC with via 1 not enclosed by metal 2. Which command gives you the marker positions?
Answers
  1. The heater is a resistive film on the same oxide surface as metal 1. If it touched metal 1, the metal would short the filament. The layout keeps 0.8 µm between them, and the DRC requires 0.6 µm.
  2. The gap is below the minimum space of 0.2 µm, so ring_modulator raises gap 0.15 um is below the minimum space of the waveguide layer. The minimum is MIN_WIDTH_SPACE in gsip_technology.py, for the layer WG.
  3. So that one via sits on the axis x = 0, where the cross-sections of the figure are cut. The test test_a_contact_via_sits_on_the_symmetry_axis checks it.
  4. DESIGN_RULES["m1_to_heater"] in gsip_technology.py. The DRC reads it, and the builder reads it for its clearance check.
  5. uv run python gsip_drc.py chip.gds writes chip_drc.lyrdb. Open the layout in KLayout and load that report in the marker browser.

Exercises #

  1. Radius 12. Run uv run python devices/ring_modulator_builder.py after changing radius to 12.0 in the ring_modulator defaults in devices/ring_modulator_builder.py. Read the lengths printed on the first line and check them against the circumference, 2π × 12 µm. Run the DRC on the component and confirm that it passes. Then ask: how much room is there now between the heater landings and the bus? Work it out from y_bus, the radius and the lowest point of the heater, and compare the result with the 0.5 µm that the builder requires. Passing at radius 12 does not tell you the margin.

  2. A gap sweep on one mask. Keep the mask fixed and change only the gap. In a Python shell at the repository root, run:

    python
    from devices import ring_modulator_builder as rm
from gsip_drc import run_drc

for gap in (0.25, 0.31, 0.34, 0.37, 0.40, 0.43, 0.52):
    print(gap, run_drc(rm.ring_modulator(gap=gap)).summary().splitlines()[0])

    All seven pass with 0 errors and 0 warnings. Then compare the gaps with the extinction in chapter 4, step 6: 46 dB at 370 nm, and about 15 dB at 340 and at 400 nm. The DRC does not measure extinction. It only says the layout is legal. This is the point of the exercise: a legal gap is not a good gap.

  3. p_inside=True. Edit nothing. Build the device with rm.ring_modulator(p_inside=True) and run the DRC. It passes. Now look at the figure in panel b of assets/ring_modulator_layout.png, and think about which side of the junction the signal disc now sits on. The DRC checks enclosure by N++ or P++ and does not check which side the signal is on. The CHARGE step of chapter 4 (step 3) would show it, because it simulates the doping as drawn. To return to the device that is simulated, pass p_inside=False, or change the default of p_inside in devices/ring_modulator_builder.py.

  4. A heater angle that the builder rejects. Build the device with pn_angle=240.0 and heater_angle=240.0. The builder raises heater_angle must exceed pn_angle by more: -1.20 um between the outer electrode and a heater landing, need 0.80 um. Explain in your own words why a heater of the same angle as the junction is refused. Then find the range of heater angles that the builder accepts for pn_angle=240.0, by trying values through ring_modulator(heater_angle=...). The lower limit is set by the electrode check, at about 259.8°. The upper limit is set by the bus check, at about 277.6°. Confirm both limits, and say which check gives each one.

  5. Your own builder. Copy the contact_arc snippet of section 7 into a new file devices/my_device_builder.py. Take the first lines, with the imports, from devices/strip_waveguide_builder.py. Add a second cell that places a row of vias on the other side of the ring, and a __main__ block that ends with write_device, as in section 8. Predict the DRC result before you run it, then check the marker file in KLayout.
