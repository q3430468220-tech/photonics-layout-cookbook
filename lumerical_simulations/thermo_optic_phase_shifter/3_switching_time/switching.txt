HEAT, two-dimensional transient, Lumerical 2025 R1: mesh 0.02 to 0.5 um, time step 2e-08 to 2e-06 s, 10 mW switched on at t = 0 for 400 us; run time 10.6 min
answer: the device has a time constant of 18.0 us and rises from 10 to 90 % in 49.9 us; without trenches 6.1 and 26.9 us.  The trenches make it 2.9 times slower and lower the power for pi 2.19 times (the cross-section with a trench on each side; step 2 has the device as drawn).

a. rise and fall of the mean core temperature
      case   tau us  10-90 % us  t_rise/tau   final K  steady K  diff %
    device    18.00       49.92        2.77    38.742    38.743   -0.00
 reference     6.12       26.91        4.39    17.681    17.681   -0.00
  tau: the rise reaches 1 - 1/e = 63 % of its final value; a single exponential would give t_rise / tau = ln 9 = 2.20.  The response is not one exponential, because heat diffuses: it starts fast and ends slowly.
  fall of the device after 400 us of power: tau 18.69 us, 90 to 10 % in 49.66 us (rise: 18.00 and 49.92 us).  The model is linear, so the fall must mirror the rise.
  It does within 0.5 % in the 10 to 90 % time; the time constant of the fall is +3.8 % off, because the solver starts the rise with its smallest time step and meets the switch-off with its largest (see c).
  0.35 / t_rise = 7.0 kHz for the device and 13.0 kHz for the reference: a rule of thumb for the bandwidth, not a simulated 3 dB point

  P_pi of a gap = 10.03 mW (step 2, fully isolated cross-section) x eta of the device / eta of that gap (step 1): a scaling
  the scaling gives 21.98 mW without trenches; step 2 solved that case and found 22.00 mW (-0.07 %)
  The product is smallest without trenches (135 mW us) and largest at 4 um (224); the device has 181.  Its trenches save power by a factor 2.19 and cost a factor 2.94 in time.
  A trench does not only keep the heat in: the heat now has to warm the whole wall of oxide before the core settles.
  With a trench the product is 1.29 to 1.66 times that of the guide without trenches: every trench of this table costs more time than it saves power.

c. checks
  time step halved (1e-08 to 1e-06 s, 904 steps against 539): tau 18.00 us (+0.02 %), 10-90 % 49.93 us (+0.02 %); time constant of the fall 18.35 us against 18.69 us: with the smaller step it lies +1.9 % from that of the rise
  run twice as long (800 us): final rise 38.735 K against 38.742 K (-0.020 %), tau 18.00 us (+0.01 %)
  final value against the steady state of step 1: device -0.00 %, reference -0.00 %; the last tenth of the rise still adds 0.001 % (device)
  by hand: between the trenches the oxide is a column h = 7.5 um high (the region of the model ends 0.2 um below the top of the cladding), cold at its foot and insulated on top and at its sides.
    Its slowest mode decays with 4 h^2 / (pi^2 alpha) = 25.8 us, with alpha = k / (rho c) = 1.38 / 1.56e+06 = 8.84e-07 m^2/s (textbook values of fused silica; the solver uses the values of the process file)
    the late part of the simulated rise, from 60 to 150 us, decays with 25.3 us (-1.9 %); the other gaps give 25.4 to 25.6 us: the same column, whatever its width; without trenches 24.1 us, and there the fit depends on its window: a layer has no slowest mode
    The time constant of the device, 18.0 us, is shorter than that: the rise is a sum of the modes of the column, and the faster ones carry part of it.

limits: a two-dimensional cross-section; the ends of the film, the metal wires and the air above the chip are not in
  the model.  The times are those of the temperature of the core; the phase follows it without delay.
